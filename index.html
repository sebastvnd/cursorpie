How it works

Hold. Flick. Release.

The actions you use most should not live in menus or in your memory. Just move your mouse or your finger.

Hold the trigger
Clipboard ✓
  1. 01

    Hold the trigger

    Hold the middle mouse button, or touch and hold your trackpad, for a split second (150 ms by default). The pie appears right under your cursor.

  2. 02

    Flick toward a slice

    Move toward the action you need. The slice lights up as your pointer passes over it. Muscle memory takes over fast.

  3. 03

    Release to act

    Let go and the action runs. Release in the center to cancel. Keep holding in the center and Settings opens after 1.2 seconds.

A clickable scroll wheel or a trackpad is all you need. Trackpad hold works on macOS, Windows and Linux; turn it on in Settings > Behavior > Trackpad.

Yours to keep

No account.
No telemetry.
Just a tiny app.

CursorPie runs on your computer and stays out of sight until you hold the trigger. The only network request it ever makes is an optional once-a-day update check to api.github.com, which you can switch off in About > Updates.

FAQ

Questions you
might have.

Still stuck? The known issues page lists what does not work yet, per system.

Is CursorPie free?

Yes. CursorPie is open source under the MIT license, with no account and no ads.

Does my data go online?

No account, no telemetry. Keyboard events are read only while you record a shortcut in Settings; otherwise events are passed through and are not stored or logged. The one network request is the optional update check: when Settings opens, at most once a day, CursorPie asks api.github.com for the newest stable release. Turn it off in About > Updates. See Updates.

Can I use it without a middle mouse button?

Yes, with a trackpad. Turn on Open the pie with a trackpad hold in Settings > Behavior > Trackpad, then touch and hold. It works on macOS, Windows (precision touchpads) and Linux, X11 or Wayland (your user must be in the input group, or on Wayland the udev rule must be installed). With a mouse, a clickable scroll wheel is enough. On macOS, some additional mouse buttons can also be triggers.

Does the trackpad feature work on my system?

It is off by default and new, so it has not been tried on every device. macOS reads any trackpad, Windows needs a precision touchpad, and Linux reads the touchpad from /dev/input. Haptic ticks exist on macOS only. See the trackpad guide.

How do I change the slices?

Hold in the center of the pie to open Settings. From there, add actions, change icons, and set up pies for specific apps.

Is Linux Wayland supported?

Yes, but it is experimental; X11 is pre-stable. On Wayland CursorPie grabs the mouse through /dev/input, so it needs the udev rule 70-cursorpie.rules that ships in the package. Per-app profiles work on KDE Plasma, Hyprland, GNOME (with the extension) and, mostly, Sway. Wayland support has not yet been run on real hardware, and a tray icon is not available yet. See the Linux guide.
