How it works

Three movements.
Nothing to memorize.

The actions you use most should not be buried in menus or take up space in your memory. Just move your mouse or your finger.

01

Hold the trigger

Hold the middle mouse button, or touch and hold your trackpad, for a split second (150 ms by default). The pie appears right under your cursor.

01
02

Flick toward a slice

Move toward the action you need. The slice highlights as your pointer passes over it.

03

Release to act

Release the button or lift your finger and the action runs. Hold in the center to open Settings.

Common questions

A few things you
might be wondering.

Is CursorPie free?

Yes. CursorPie is open source under the MIT license, with no account and no ads.

Does my data go online?

No. CursorPie does not make network connections or collect telemetry. Keyboard events are read while you record a shortcut in Settings; otherwise, events are passed through and are not stored or logged.

Can I use it without a middle mouse button?

Yes, with a trackpad. Turn on Open the pie with a trackpad hold in Settings > Behavior > Trackpad, then touch and hold. It works on macOS, Windows (precision touchpads) and Linux, X11 or Wayland (your user must be in the input group, or on Wayland the udev rule must be installed). With a mouse, a clickable scroll wheel is enough. On macOS, some additional mouse buttons can also be used as triggers.

Does the trackpad feature work on my system?

It is off by default and new, so it has not been tried on every device. macOS reads any trackpad, Windows needs a precision touchpad, and Linux reads the touchpad from /dev/input. Haptic ticks exist on macOS only. See the known issues.

How do I change the slices?

Hold in the center of the pie to open Settings. From there, add actions, change icons, and configure pies for specific apps.

Is Linux Wayland supported?

Yes, but it is experimental; X11 is pre-stable. CursorPie works in X11 and in Wayland sessions. On Wayland it grabs the mouse through /dev/input, so it needs the udev rule 70-cursorpie.rules that ships in the package; per-app profiles work on KDE Plasma, Hyprland, GNOME (with the extension) and, mostly, Sway. Wayland support has not yet been run on real hardware, and a tray icon is not available yet. See the Linux guide.
